# Content Engine — House Style

Shared style rules for all Content Engine skills (`linkedin`, `short-video`, `long-video`, `newsletter`). Each skill references this file instead of duplicating the rules.

## Voice

- **Use the user's words.** Lift phrases directly from the transcript wherever they sound natural. Do not paraphrase good quotes — that is the whole point of working from a transcript.
- **Second person.** "You", not "we", "people", or "everyone".
- **One idea per piece.** A 10-minute transcript usually contains 3-5 ideas. Pick one and hit it hard.
- **Sounds like a real person.** If a business owner would not say it out loud, do not write it.

## Mechanics

- **British English.** Organisation, realise, colour, behaviour, optimise.
- **No em-dashes.** Use hyphens, commas, or restructure the sentence.
- **Short paragraphs.** 1-3 sentences. Walls of text lose readers.
- **Short sentences.** Especially in spoken formats (short-video, long-video). Under 15 words is a good ceiling.
- **Hashtags only at the end**, never in the body. Skills that produce video scripts or newsletters use no hashtags at all.

## Banned hype words

Never use these. They are AI tells.

- insane, mind-blowing, game-changer, revolutionary
- unlock, empower, leverage, synergy
- seamless, cutting-edge, robust, holistic

## Banned AI phrases

- "in today's fast-paced world"
- "it is crucial to"
- "let us explore" / "let us dive into"
- "dive into"
- "navigate the complexities"
- "the ultimate guide to"
- "without further ado"

## Why these rules exist

Most AI-written content sounds like AI because it starts from a blank page with a topic. The model invents content with no opinion, no voice, and no specific perspective, then reaches for the same hype words and transitions every time.

Starting from a transcript means the opinion, voice, and examples are already the user's. The skill's job is to compress and shape, not to create. These rules exist to stop the model from "improving" the user's voice into a generic content-farm tone.
